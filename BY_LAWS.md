## Definitions

* **Bylaws**: The present rules governing the Thunderbird Council.
* **Council**: The Thunderbird Council composed of elected members of the Thunderbird project
* **Members**: the term Members signifies all people recognized as such by the present Bylaws.
* **TBTC**: Thunderbird Technology Corporation
* **Thunderbird**: The Thunderbird project.
* **CPG**: [Mozilla Community Participation Guidelines](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/about/governance/policies/participation/)
* **Active Councilors**: Councilors occupying non-vacant Council seats; a temporary absence or recusal does not create a vacancy.
* **Community member**: A person who uses, contributes to, or participates in the Thunderbird project in good faith; only qualified electors may vote in Council elections.
* **Electors**: Eligible contributors admitted to the verified election roll.
* **ABSTAIN**: An express choice not to vote for or against a motion. An abstention counts toward participation quorum but is excluded from the ordinary-majority denominator.
* **RECUSED**: A Councilor excluded from an affected decision under the conflicts of interest procedure; unlike an abstention, a recusal is not a vote.
* **Ordinary majority**: More YES than NO votes among Councilors voting YES or NO on a motion, provided the applicable quorum is satisfied. ABSTAIN is not a NO vote and does not count in the majority denominator.
* **Supermajority of active Councilors**: The stated fraction of all occupied Council seats, rounded up; abstentions, recusals, and absences do not lower that threshold.

## Mandate

The Thunderbird Council is the governing body for the Thunderbird project. The detailed duties and powers of the Thunderbird Council are described in the [Thunderbird Operational Rules Term Sheet](https://github.com/thunderbird/council-docs/blob/main/TERM_SHEET.md).

The Council shall maintain a forward calendar to exercise its responsibilities under the Term Sheet, including annual project goals, roadmap approval, initial budget approval, quarterly product and financial oversight, and its role concerning staff leadership goals. The Council's internal procedures do not expand its corporate authority, alter TBTC's day-to-day management responsibilities, or override the Term Sheet's approval and dispute-resolution provisions. The Council shall document important unmet oversight obligations and next steps in its Community updates where not confidential.

## Election Procedure

The Thunderbird Council shall meet immediately after their election for the purpose of electing its new officers.

The current election process is detailed below.

1. A list of persons eligible to vote (the "electors") shall be established by the existing Thunderbird Council, based on “[Membership Eligibility](#eligibility)” below. The election observer shall have access to the verified voter roll. Electors may choose whether their names or a verified public pseudonym appear on the public roll; identities and eligibility must remain auditable to the observer without publishing private email addresses or contribution records.
2. The list of electors will be entered into a private mailing list (currently known as tb-election) or comparably accessible election forum. All electors shall receive the same voting instructions and candidate materials. Candidate discussion shall be available to the electorate and may also occur in open Community channels, with appropriate privacy protection and consistent moderation for abuse under the CPG. Electors must have an accessible way to ask questions of every candidate.
3. Eligible contributors seeking election shall submit a candidacy statement containing:
    1. their name or publicly used contributor identity;
    2. current professional affiliations and material relevant conflicts;
    3. an inclusive summary of their Thunderbird contributions; and
    4. their motivation, priorities, and approach to representing the Community.

   The election manager shall publish a common, reasonably concise candidate questionnaire at least two weeks before nominations. The questionnaire may be updated before each election with Community input and observer review; every candidate receives the same questions, comparable space, and a reasonable opportunity to answer follow-up questions.
4. The election manager shall acknowledge receipt of candidacies and make application instructions and UTC deadlines clear. A reasonable cure period shall be allowed for demonstrable technical delivery failures, accessibility barriers, or administrative errors, as determined impartially by the election observer. Candidates and electors may appeal eligibility or administrative decisions to the observer before voting begins.
5. In the case there are more than two people nominated from the same professional affiliation, a pre-election will take place first and the two winners will continue to the real election, unless a verified integrated affiliation-cap method is adopted under item 6. No single organization or company will be allowed to control more than 2 seats of the Council, regardless of election results. A parent organization and entities it controls, including subsidiaries and organizations under common control, are one professional affiliation for this limit. Mozilla Foundation, Mozilla Corporation, and Thunderbird Technology Corporation (formerly MZLA Technologies Corporation) are treated as one such affiliation. Unpaid participation in another Mozilla project, without another qualifying relationship, does not by itself create that affiliation.

   The organization-wide seat limit shall be verified by the neutral election observer and enforced in elections and appointments. Changes to the definition of common affiliation take effect for the next regular Council election and do not terminate existing elected terms. If an affiliation changes during a term, the Council shall document a fair transition and address excess seats at the next election or vacancy rather than require an immediate resignation solely because of the change.

   Individuals affiliated with a company or organization are people who are employees, officers, or members of the board of directors of an organization; or have a significant consulting relationship; or own at least 1% of the equity or debt, or derivatives thereof, of a company.
6. Electors shall vote for Thunderbird Council members from the list of those standing for election, with the specific method and process selected by a neutral third-party observer. The method, eligibility standards, affiliation-limit enforcement, counting rules, and any recount or appeal process shall be published before voting. The observer may approve a well-specified, auditable affiliation-constrained Scottish STV count in place of a separate affiliation pre-election, but only if its implementation and consequences are verified and announced before nominations; otherwise the pre-election rule in item 5 applies.
7. The voting process shall select a Thunderbird Council of seven members, each with a two-year term as described in sections “[Composition](#composition)” and “[Term](#term)”. Voting will occur on an external, secure voting-as-a-service platform. If more than seven people stand for election, voting will be done using “ranked choice”, with conversion of the individual choices into an elected Thunderbird Council done using the Scottish STV method.
8. If the number of candidates does not exceed the available seats, electors shall vote YES or NO on each candidate separately. A candidate is elected only with more YES than NO valid votes cast for that candidate, subject to the organizational-affiliation limit. Unfilled seats shall be treated as vacancies under these bylaws. An invalidated election or a ballot with no elected Councilors shall be repeated under the neutral observer's supervision.

### Thunderbird Project Election Observer

#### Description:

The primary functions of the election "observer" during the election process are as follows:

1. Be an impartial party.
2. Privately or publicly raise concerns if any person - participant, election manager, or Thunderbird Council - acts improperly. And may also directly communicate with the Thunderbird Council and the public.
3. Approve the documented election process as proposed by the Thunderbird Council as being fair and correct.
4. Assures that the documented election process is actually properly applied throughout the election period.
5. Works closely with the Thunderbird Council appointed election manager(s).
6. The observer should setup, run and moderate the election mailing list to be able to fulfill their responsibility of observing and ensuring the process is properly and fairly applied. They may request the assistance of Thunderbird Council appointed “manager(s)” to setup, build, implement, and manage the election process, but the observer must have all the powers that the managers have AND be able to see all the moderation activity, all emails and all communications - to be able to fulfill their responsibility of observing and ensuring the process is properly and fairly applied.
7. (In this step, the observer is the primary driver) Be the ONLY person to administer the voting service, and run the vote. There must be no possibility for any other person to influence the election.
8. Announces the vote result.
9. Publish an election report stating voter participation, vote-counting method, applicable affiliation caps, results, and any material procedural challenges, without revealing secret ballots.
10. Provide an impartial process for timely eligibility disputes, recount requests, and election complaints, and document their disposition subject to privacy protections.

It should also be noted that the CPG is to be observed throughout the process. It is not the observer's job to enforce the CPG, but it would be entirely proper to be involved in determining if CPG violations have occurred. The observer will need to engage with the CPG team if and when necessary during the process.

#### The number of hours:

This depends on the amount of discussion and controversy, if any, during the election period.

Typically it takes 5–10 hours in the 5–6 week period, if there are no election hurdles, but may take up to 15–20 hours

#### Pay:

This is a voluntary position, with non-monetary compensation.

### Election timetable

The election process will start on the first Monday of March every 2 years.

Election administrators shall publish the complete timetable in UTC and in accessible formats well in advance, confirm receipt of registrations and nominations, and provide reasonable accommodations for language, accessibility, and time zones. Recruitment and outreach shall specifically welcome nontechnical contributors, independent volunteers, localizers, support contributors, add-on developers, designers, testers, and other historically underrepresented Community participants. All candidates are subject to identical eligibility rules.

The election timetable shall run as follows:

1. Electoral roll created and published (January)
2. Announcement of the election on first Monday of March
3. Nominations to the Council (1 week)
4. Discussion period (1 week)
5. Pre-election voting period (1 week)
6. Voting period (1 week)
7. Results announced

## Membership

### Eligibility

Contributors are eligible to vote in Thunderbird Council elections if they fulfill the following requirements:

Elector qualification is 20 hours of contribution within the previous two years. Qualifying activity includes code and add-on development, review, testing, localization, support, documentation, design, accessibility, community organizing and moderation, advocacy, research, outreach, and governance work. Contributions need not be made through Mozilla-hosted development platforms. The election manager shall publish proportionate evidence guidelines and an impartial review or appeal mechanism for difficult-to-document contributions. Contributors shall be evaluated under consistent criteria, without favoring paid employment or technical work.

To avoid conflicting duties from multiple directorships, members of the board of directors of Mozilla Foundation, Mozilla Corporation, and Thunderbird Technology Corporation may not serve on the Thunderbird Council.

### Renewal

Contributors must renew their membership before each election during the month of January. Their membership renewal must contain:

1. Name;
2. Email;
3. Current professional affiliation (if any);
4. A statement summarizing their contributions to Thunderbird, with links or other reasonable evidence where available (for example ATN, SUMO, Pontoon, Bugzilla, community forums, support or outreach activity). No particular platform account is required.

## Thunderbird Council

### Composition

The Thunderbird Council is composed of nominally seven (7) members elected at the Election every two (2) years. There must be a minimum of four (4) members. The number of active members is the number of non-vacant seats on the Council.

### Term

Each councilor is elected for a two year term during the council election.

### Roles

Council roles are elected by the Council. Each role has a 1-year term. The Chair and Secretary are facilitators accountable to the full Council; they do not hold a veto over Councilor initiatives, eligible agenda items, or duly adopted decisions. Officers may be replaced before their term ends by an ordinary motion following notice and an opportunity for response, with the affected officer abstaining or recusing as required under the conflicts policy. The Council shall maintain a written handover of outstanding motions, decisions, and responsibilities.

#### Chairperson

Organize the work of the Thunderbird Council and encourage members to do the same

1. Working with TBTC to approve annual budgets
2. Primary interface with TBTC
3. Provide direction to the high level TBTC staff
4. Primary spokesperson for the project
5. Update the community about Thunderbird Council progress
6. Ensure proper handover to the new Thunderbird Council, all unresolved issues/tasks from prior term are transferred to the new Thunderbird Council
7. Represent the Thunderbird Council in TBTC Quarterly Board meetings
8. Prepare the agenda based on the input of other Councilors and run meetings impartially, without blocking motions or suppressing minority views; present agreed Council positions as such and distinguish personal views

#### Secretary

1. Run and record Thunderbird Council votes
2. Prepare and announce meetings
3. Ensure elections take place
4. Acting-chairperson in their absence

#### Councilors

1. Organize, promote and foster the Thunderbird project & community
2. Plan and develop the future of the Thunderbird product and community
3. Oversee at a high-level the structure and well-being of the Thunderbird project
4. Improve the structure and operation of the Thunderbird Council
5. Lead and participate in Thunderbird Council projects
6. Spokesperson for the project
7. Interface with TBTC, using reasonable coordination so that staff receive clear and manageable requests
8. If chairperson and secretary are not present, a member may act as the chairperson for the duration of the meeting after a procedural vote.
9. Independently research, consult, collect feedback, develop drafts, and propose initiatives on matters within the Council's mandate without prior Council approval, provided the Councilor makes no unauthorized commitment on behalf of the Council or TBTC.
10. Obtain reasonably necessary information and equal access to Council proceedings, subject to legal confidentiality, privacy, conflict restrictions, and the Term Sheet. Individual Councilors may express personal views publicly while clearly distinguishing them from official Council decisions.

### Compensation

Councilors will not be paid for their services. However, the Thunderbird Council may, at any time, resolve to reimburse Councilors for expenses incurred in the exercise of their functions. The Council shall maintain an equitable, published reimbursement and advance-payment policy, subject to an approved budget and applicable TBTC rules, so that Councilors need not ordinarily personally finance required Council activities. This includes reasonable work-related equipment, communications, accessibility and travel costs, with comparable access for employee and non-employee Councilors and no double reimbursement.

### Indemnity

Members of the Council are indemnified under the bylaws of Thunderbird Technology Corporation in their capacities as agents of the corporation.

### Vacancies

A vacancy is created whenever there are fewer than the nominal number of Council members, including, but not limited to, an election resulting in fewer than the nominal number of members, a resignation or death of a Council member.

Vacancies are filled by appointment of 2/3rds vote of active Council members. An appointed Council member must have a valid membership and should serve until the current term is over.

### Resignation

A Council member may resign by presenting their resignation in writing to the Thunderbird Council, which shall be effective upon acceptance by the Thunderbird Council.

An ordinary resignation may not be unreasonably withheld; acceptance shall normally be recorded within seven days. A Councilor who cannot participate for an extended period should notify the Secretary, who shall discuss reasonable accommodations and succession arrangements without treating temporary absence as an automatic vacancy.


### Individual Initiatives and Working Groups

A Councilor may establish and participate in an informal exploratory group with interested Community members, without prior Council approval, to research an issue and prepare recommendations. Such a group has no authority to speak for the Council, receive confidential Council or TBTC information, direct staff, commit project resources, or approve decisions. Formal Council Working Groups exercising delegated responsibilities require a published charter approved by the Council defining scope, participants, reporting, duration or review date, and limits of authority. No working group replaces a decision reserved to the full Council or TBTC under the Term Sheet. Reasonable opportunities for Community participation should be provided where appropriate.

## Call for re-election

The Council may pass a motion for an immediate election to occur, including why the Council feels that a new election must occur. For the motion to pass, 2/3rds of the active Council members must vote in favor. The current Council remains in power until the election procedure has finished.

## Dissolution

If the Council wishes to dissolve, the Council must adopt a motion recommending for the Council to be dissolved. This motion must have support from at least 75% of the Councilors. If the motion is adopted, the Council must organize a community referendum. During this referendum, all members of the electorate may vote on the resolution to dissolve the Council. A majority of 2/3 of the electorate is needed for the dissolution to occur.

## Meetings

### Quorum

Quorum for a meeting is more than half of the active Councilors, rounded up. For a vote on a particular matter, participation quorum is more than half of the Councilors eligible to vote on that matter, rounded up; formally recused Councilors are excluded from this participation-quorum denominator. An asynchronous vote meets quorum only when at least that many eligible Councilors cast YES, NO, or ABSTAIN; a synchronous vote additionally requires a meeting quorum present. If fewer than three Councilors remain eligible, the Council shall obtain independent advice and postpone the substantive decision until a lawful, fair process is available. Recusal does not reduce any threshold expressly based on all active Councilors.

For synchronous votes, the meeting quorum must be present when the vote is taken. A meeting quorum does not substitute for the separate participation requirement for an asynchronous vote. A Councilor attending a hybrid meeting live may participate in a synchronous vote.

### Frequency

The Council shall hold regularly scheduled meetings at least once a month.

Regular meetings should prioritize decisions and substantive discussion. Routine updates should be circulated in writing in advance. The Chair shall propose reasonable, evenhanded speaking limits (normally two minutes for an initial contribution and one minute per subsequent contribution), give priority to those who have not spoken, and seek consent or a procedural vote for extensions. Members may contribute in writing without these oral time limits. Accommodations for accessibility, language, and complex subjects shall be made.

At least one-third of the time of an ordinary meeting should normally be reserved for pending motions and decisions unless the Council agrees otherwise. Multiple motions may be discussed or voted on in one meeting. Uncontroversial procedural items may be grouped into a consent agenda, but any Councilor can require separate consideration; formal recorded votes and thresholds cannot be bypassed.

### Agenda

1. Every Councilor may submit agenda items before a meeting. Timely, in-scope items must appear on the published agenda or pending-agenda list, with a reason and proposed date if deferred. The Chair may prioritize urgent Term Sheet obligations but shall not unilaterally suppress an item.
2. The Secretary shall circulate a draft agenda and relevant materials where practicable at least 48 hours in advance. On request, the agenda may be adapted by a procedural vote at the beginning of the meeting.
3. Unresolved agenda items shall identify an owner, next step, and target review date, without automatically expiring.

### Participants

External participants may be invited to attend Council meetings.

Ordinary Council meetings may include Community-accessible portions. Confidential executive sessions must have a specified reason consistent with the [Confidentiality](#confidentiality) section; public agendas and minutes should describe their general subject and any non-confidential outcome.

### Minutes

The Thunderbird Council shall keep written minutes of its proceedings in its permanent records. The minutes will include, at the least, the date and time of the meeting, the names of all members present, a fair summary of the principal points discussed, motions proposed and voted upon, action items and owners, and any Councilor objections to motions/resolutions. On any Councilor's request, the Secretary shall include a fair summary of any specified part or all of a relevant asynchronous Council discussion in the next meeting notes and publish that summary as part of the next Community update, redacting only protected information. The requesting Councilor may specify the subjects to be covered; the summary need not reproduce the entire discussion verbatim. Draft minutes should be circulated for correction and a redacted public version published normally within seven days, and in any event before the next regular meeting. The Secretary shall maintain links between minutes, decisions, and the motion register; necessary redactions shall be indicated wherever the existence of the redaction may safely be disclosed.

### Community Updates

It is the duty of the Council to update the Community about ongoing discussions, allowing the community to participate and weigh in on a regular basis to encourage community participation. These updates must be free from confidential information, see [Confidentiality](#confidentiality).

A summary of the meeting shall be shared with the Thunderbird community before the next regularly scheduled meeting. The Council shall make public channels for proposals and feedback reasonably accessible, and shall publish an annual activity report covering Council goals, decisions, progress on Term Sheet oversight, working groups, election participation, and high-level budget stewardship, subject to appropriate confidentiality safeguards.

For a proposed material change to Council membership, elections, the Council's governing authority, or the Term Sheet, the Council shall ordinarily publish the proposed changes for at least 14 days of Community comment before final approval and summarize material feedback and its disposition. Other important proposals, including annual roadmaps and budgets, shall invite Community input early enough to be considered meaningfully. Confidential or genuinely urgent matters may use a documented exception, without bypassing mandatory bylaw or Term Sheet approvals.

## Decision-Making Process

The Council is empowered to make decisions representing the Thunderbird project and should act in the best interest of the Thunderbird project.

### Voting

The Chairperson does not have any additional powers nor extra votes. Any references to Chair correspond to Chair or acting-Chair; and references to secretary correspond to the secretary or Chair.

### Motion

A motion is a written proposal submitted at any time by a Council member.

**Community proposals:** Any Thunderbird Community member may submit a proposal through a publicly documented intake channel. The Council shall acknowledge it and publish a non-confidential status. A Councilor may sponsor it as a formal motion; two Councilors' sponsorship or seconding triggers the ordinary discussion process. Proposals not sponsored shall receive a brief public disposition where practicable, but do not automatically require a Council vote. The intake process shall apply impartial moderation, confidentiality, and anti-spam rules.

**Second:** Another Councilor may second a motion to allow its formal discussion. A second does not imply support for the motion. An unseconded motion remains pending until seconded or withdrawn by its proposer; it does not expire automatically.

**Pending motions:** The Secretary shall maintain a complete register accessible to all Councilors by default, including all unseconded motions. Neither an interface nor an administrative practice may hide motions older than two months by default. Pending motions must not be automatically closed, removed, or treated as withdrawn merely because they have not yet received a second, discussion, or a vote. The Secretary shall circulate a reminder of motions awaiting a second at least once every 30 days, including a link to the full register. Non-confidential pending motions, including unseconded proposals, shall also be listed in a public register with their current status and last substantive update.

**Discussion:** Each seconded motion must receive a genuine opportunity for deliberation, amendment, and consultation before voting. The motion and relevant supporting materials must be available to all Councilors for at least 72 hours before voting, during which members may consider the proposal, provide comments, and propose amendments. A motion principally discussed at a meeting may be debated and voted on at the same meeting once that minimum period has elapsed. For consequential matters (including budgets, roadmaps, governance, and significant commitments), the default consideration period is at least seven days unless all eligible Councilors agree that the matter is ready sooner, but never less than the mandatory 72 hours. Applicable longer notice periods, including for bylaw amendments, continue to apply. Councilors may request a reasonable extension to prepare amendments, obtain material information, consult the Community, or negotiate with TBTC; an extension request and disposition shall be recorded.

**Amendments:** Any Councilor may propose a written amendment during discussion. The proposer may incorporate an unopposed amendment; contested amendments shall be discussed and, where necessary, decided by an ordinary procedural vote before the final motion. A vote cannot begin while a duly submitted amendment remains unresolved.

**Postponement and follow-up:** A motion may be postponed by the proposer or a procedural vote, stating an owner or reason and a next review date. Postponement does not cause expiration. The Council shall maintain a status and action register for unresolved items. The proposer may withdraw a motion before voting; after voting begins, withdrawal requires Council consent.

**Call for vote:** After the applicable discussion period, the Chair shall schedule an asynchronous vote within seven days of a request by the proposer or another Councilor, or place a synchronous vote on the agenda of the next regular meeting (or a reasonably scheduled special meeting), unless the Council agrees to a documented postponement. Any Councilor may request a procedural vote on scheduling a vote; a passing procedural vote obliges the Chair or Secretary to schedule it, and cannot dispense with mandatory notice or discussion. Closing discussion early over an objection requires two-thirds of active Councilors, and never overrides minimum notice or review periods. Any Councilor may raise a point of order or appeal a procedural ruling to the Council; the Chair shall state the ruling and an appeal is decided by an ordinary procedural vote.

**Form of discussion and voting:** A motion substantively discussed at an in-person Council meeting shall be voted on at an in-person Council meeting; one discussed at a live remote Council meeting shall be voted on live at a Council meeting; and one discussed asynchronously shall be voted on asynchronously. Voting may occur at a later meeting if further preparation is needed. If substantive discussion occurs in more than one format, the Council shall choose the voting format by an ordinary procedural vote before opening the final vote, using a fair process that does not exclude members merely to influence the outcome. Distribution of documents and incidental written questions alone do not change the principal discussion format.

**Final text and notice:** The Chair or Secretary shall provide every Councilor the identical, final, dated text of the motion, supporting materials, voting format, applicable deadline (if asynchronous), and threshold before opening the vote. An asynchronous vote shall remain open for at least 24 hours and at most seven days; a meeting vote is conducted and completed during the meeting with quorum present. An expressly urgent operational matter may use a shorter discussion period only with a two-thirds vote of active Councilors, and never where these bylaws or the Term Sheet impose longer notice or approval requirements.

**Voting:** Every Councilor eligible to vote must be notified and may vote YES, NO, or ABSTAIN. For final votes on motions and contested amendments, individual ballots and running totals must remain confidential from other Councilors until voting closes; the Secretary or a neutral administrator may access only what is necessary to administer the vote. At an in-person or live meeting, use simultaneous confidential paper or electronic ballots, collected and counted after participating Councilors have voted; ordinary procedural decisions may be taken by general consent or an open vote unless a Councilor requests a confidential ballot. For asynchronous votes, a Councilor may update a ballot before closing under the same procedure available to everyone, and voting closes at the published deadline or when all eligible Councilors have cast a ballot, whichever comes first. For meeting votes, voting closes after all participating eligible Councilors have had an opportunity to cast a ballot. Only then shall the results and each Councilor's vote be released together and published as provided below. A recusal is recorded separately and is not a ballot.

**Counting:** An ordinary motion passes when YES votes outnumber NO votes and the applicable quorum is satisfied. ABSTAIN does not count as NO or increase the ordinary majority denominator. A tied vote fails. Rules requiring an absolute majority or a fraction of active Councilors, including the two-thirds requirement to amend the bylaws, remain unchanged by abstentions. The Secretary shall record the eligible voters, quorum, votes, recusals, threshold, and outcome.

The secretary shall ensure that completed votes and motions are recorded in the minutes and shared publicly after redacting confidential or sensitive information. The complete public list of Council motions shall be published before the next regularly scheduled meeting including the final motion, date, proposer, seconder, voting threshold, how each Councilor voted or was recused, and the result. The public register shall also include non-confidential pending motions and their statuses, available amendments, supporting materials, and implementation updates.

Any Councilor may provide one optional explanatory statement of up to 100 words for their YES, NO, or ABSTAIN vote, alone or jointly with others. Statements shall be published only with the completed voting record, not while voting is open. Statements must follow the CPG and omit protected information; review and redaction shall be limited to confidentiality, objectively verifiable factual corrections, and conduct requirements, not disagreement with the Councilor's view. An omitted statement does not delay or invalidate a vote.

## Conflicts of Interest

### Purpose and scope

The Council shall act in Thunderbird's interests and avoid decisions improperly influenced by Councilors' private interests. A conflict may be actual, potential, or reasonably perceived, and may arise from employment, consulting, ownership, gifts, close family relationships, leadership roles, or other material financial or non-financial interests. Ordinary membership in the Thunderbird Community, an openly stated opinion, or employment by TBTC does not by itself require recusal from all Thunderbird matters. This policy supplements, and does not replace, applicable law or existing fiduciary and confidentiality obligations.

### Disclosure and independence

1. On entering office and once each year, Councilors shall privately disclose to the Secretary relevant employers, significant consulting and ownership relationships, leadership roles, and close personal relationships that may materially affect Council business; disclosures shall be updated when circumstances change. Financial interests include direct or indirect compensation, prospective arrangements, material investments, and gifts or favors from interested parties exceeding US$200 in aggregate per year. The US$200 amount is a disclosure guide, not permission to accept an improper benefit. Councilors need not disclose political or religious beliefs or private friendships unless a specific, material interest creates an actual conflict.
2. Before consideration of an affected matter, a Councilor shall disclose a relevant interest to the Council and may explain the circumstances. Any Councilor may confidentially request review. Voluntary recusal does not require a finding of wrongdoing.
3. A Councilor may be described as **independent of TBTC** for reporting purposes when they have no current or recent (within 18 months) employment, significant paid consulting, material financial interest, or close-family executive relationship with TBTC or its controlled affiliates. This descriptive classification does not by itself affect eligibility, voting rights, or equal Councilor status.
4. The Secretary shall maintain a protected disclosure register and a public summary identifying relevant affiliations, the existence and general nature of material conflicts, and recusals. Personal financial details, sensitive family information, and confidential business facts shall be redacted.

### Review and recusal

1. The disinterested Councilors shall decide by ordinary majority, after hearing the affected Councilor's explanation, whether a particular conflict warrants recusal or other safeguards. The affected Councilor shall not vote on that determination. Where the Chair or Secretary is interested, another disinterested Councilor shall administer the review. If too few disinterested Councilors remain for a credible determination, the Council shall seek advice from a neutral, independent reviewer and record how it resolves the issue.
2. When recusal is required, the affected Councilor may provide relevant facts but shall not participate in deliberation or voting on the affected decision, and shall not access restricted material beyond what is necessary for fair review. Recusals shall be recorded separately from ABSTAIN votes. The disinterested Councilors shall consider reasonable alternatives to a conflicted transaction before recommending its approval.
3. Any transaction materially benefiting a Councilor or related party must be documented as fair and in Thunderbird's interests, with any required TBTC corporate approvals obtained separately. The Council's vote cannot authorize spending, contractual commitments, or corporate decisions outside its authority under the Term Sheet.

### Violations and review

A member accused of nondisclosure or misconduct shall receive written notice, an opportunity to respond, and an impartial review. Proportionate outcomes may include corrected disclosure, an independent review, mediation, a formal warning, or recusal. Suspension, removal, and Community Participation Guidelines enforcement require their own applicable safeguards and procedures; this article alone does not permit the Chair or a simple majority to remove an elected Councilor. Material findings and corrective action shall be recorded in a privacy-respecting form. A Councilor may request reconsideration where material new information emerges.

## Confidentiality

The Council shall conduct its activities openly by default. Agendas, motions, decisions, and non-confidential deliberations should be available to the Thunderbird Community. Confidential discussion or a closed session is appropriate when reasonably necessary to protect personal information, security, legal privilege, sensitive negotiations, protected business information, or binding confidentiality obligations. The Council shall document the reason for closing an item and publish an appropriate redacted outcome whenever possible.

Upon joining the Thunderbird Council, each Councilor, including elected TBTC staff, shall enter into the confidentiality arrangements necessary to access TBTC proprietary information. Nothing in these bylaws overrides an existing NDA, legal privilege, or applicable law. The Council shall work with TBTC to establish practical information-classification, redaction, and release procedures that permit greater transparency without requiring unlawful or unauthorized disclosure.

As defined by the [Thunderbird Operational Rules Term Sheet](https://github.com/thunderbird/council-docs/blob/main/TERM_SHEET.md), Council members shall have access to information related to the Thunderbird project, including to TBTC proprietary information, but will not have access to personal information of individual employees.

Council members must use official communication channels, such as their thunderbird.net email address, when performing Council activities. Thunderbird.net emails are only for use while a member is actively on the Council.

## Code of Conduct

The Thunderbird Council welcomes discussion, constructive criticism, and contributions from all members and aims to ensure that the effort being made by Council members is respected. The Thunderbird Council agrees to adhere to the [Mozilla Community Participation Guidelines](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/about/governance/policies/participation/).

As a group, we are all responsible for the speed of progress and need to hold each other accountable in a respectful way.

Councilors shall communicate respectfully and distinguish personal opinions from formal Council decisions. Good-faith public criticism of Council processes or decisions is permitted, subject to confidentiality obligations and the CPG; disagreement alone is not misconduct.

## Internal Dispute Resolution

Councilors should first seek to resolve procedural or governance disputes through documented discussion and, where appropriate, mediation. A Councilor may request reconsideration of a disputed procedural ruling by the full Council; the person whose conduct or interest is challenged shall not decide their own challenge. For significant unresolved procedural, conflict-of-interest, or election disputes, the Council shall obtain a neutral review and publish a redacted outcome. This internal process does not replace the Mozilla CPG procedures, applicable law, or the Council–TBTC dispute resolution mechanism in the Term Sheet.

## Amendments to these bylaws

Modifying the bylaws follows the normal Decision-Making Process of the Council, with the following changes:

- Proposed changes to the bylaws shall be distributed to all Councilors in writing at least fourteen (14) days before the call to vote. Material amendments to the text during that period shall be circulated with sufficient time to satisfy the same notice requirement before voting.
- Modifying the bylaws requires affirmative votes from at least two thirds (2/3) of all active Thunderbird Councilors, rounded up; abstentions, recusals, and absences do not lower this threshold.
- At least once each two-year Council term, the Council shall review the bylaws and governance procedures, solicit Community feedback, and publish findings and proposed improvements, even if it adopts no changes. Councilors may propose amendments at any time under this section.

## Separability of Articles

In case any of the provisions contained in these bylaws or any application thereof shall be invalid, illegal or unenforceable in any respect, the validity, legality, and enforceability of the remaining provisions contained in this agreement shall not in any way be affected.
